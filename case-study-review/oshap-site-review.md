## 1. Executive verdict

The short page now reads as one tight story: a real risk, a business reason, three decisions with their costs, a reversal, and an honest account of what is not known yet. The Impact box only claims what the system guarantees, which builds trust with fintech readers who notice overclaiming. What holds it below Strong is the same thing as before and is outside the page's control: no evidence from guests, staff or the restaurant, because the pilot has not opened. Two smaller gaps remain: the plan claim is not shown anywhere, and the page never says which decision mattered most. At Mid level this is a solid, interview-ready case study.

## 3. What works

1. **A risk with people in it and a business reason**: the guest who suffers, the owner who never notices, and a premium-plan feature.
2. **Decisions with costs**: each decision says what was given up, not just what shipped.
3. **Honest outcomes**: the page claims only what its tests prove and labels the rest as targets.

## 4. What isn't working

### Severity: Medium: No evidence from people
- **Evidence:** "nothing has been measured with guests or staff yet" (Impact)
- **Why it matters:** At Mid this is acceptable when stated honestly, which it is. It is still what separates Promising from Strong.
- **Fix:** Update the page with the pilot's first numbers or one real observation.

### Severity: Low: The plan claim is unsupported on the page
- **Evidence:** "only available on Oshap's highest subscription plan"
- **Why it matters:** An interviewer may ask which plan and what it costs.
- **Fix:** Name the plan, and make sure that the backend or pricing setup enforces it, because the frontend code does not.

### Severity: Low: No ranking of the decisions
- **Evidence:** Three decisions, and one I reversed
- **Why it matters:** Ranking shows judgment about what mattered.
- **Fix:** One line naming the decision you would defend hardest.

## 10. Hiring manager questions

1. Which plan includes brand colour, and has any restaurant upgraded for it?
2. How does the 216-colour sweep work, and what happens if a colour fails?
3. Why did you weigh Material 3 and Apple above the Mobbin research?
4. Which of the three decisions would you defend hardest, and why?
5. When the pilot opens, what will tell you the design system worked, rather than the product?

## 11. Final recommendation

The three changes most likely to improve hiring readiness:

1. Add the pilot's first numbers or one real observation when the restaurant opens.
2. Name the plan that includes brand colour.
3. Add one line on the decision you would defend hardest.
